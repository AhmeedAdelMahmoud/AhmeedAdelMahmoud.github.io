  <h1 id="home-title">Geometric architectures for fault-tolerant quantum information.</h1>
  <p class="home-lead">I am a Ph.D. researcher at the University of Saskatchewan working at the intersection of quantum information, topological quantum computing, quantum simulation, and quantum geometry.</p>
  <p class="home-summary">My research develops geometric and computational frameworks for quantum error correction and fault-tolerant measurement-based quantum computing, with particular emphasis on hyperbolic codes, scalable numerical simulation, and experimentally realizable emulations of quantum systems in curved geometries.</p>

<section class="home-section" aria-labelledby="directions-title">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="section-kicker">Research directions</p>
    <h2 id="directions-title">From mathematical structure to physical implementation</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="research-grid">
    <article class="research-card">
      <span class="card-index">01</span>
      <h3>Hyperbolic quantum codes</h3>
      <p>Constructing and benchmarking topological CSS and Floquet codes on periodic hyperbolic lattices, with an emphasis on scalable decoding and threshold estimation.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <span class="card-index">02</span>
      <h3>Fault-tolerant MBQC</h3>
      <p>Developing hyperbolic cluster-state architectures for fault-tolerant measurement-based quantum computing and resource-efficient logical computation.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <span class="card-index">03</span>
      <h3>Quantum simulation in curved space</h3>
      <p>Designing scalable superconducting-circuit platforms that emulate quantum systems on hyperbolic and other non-Euclidean geometries.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>
