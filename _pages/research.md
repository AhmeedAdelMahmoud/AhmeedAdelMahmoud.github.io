<header class="page-intro">
  <p class="section-kicker">Research</p>
  <h1>Geometry as a resource for quantum information</h1>
  <p>I develop geometric and computational frameworks for quantum error correction and fault-tolerant quantum computation, and connect them to experimentally realizable quantum systems.</p>
</header>

<section class="research-feature" aria-labelledby="codes-heading">
  <div class="research-feature__label"><span>01</span><p>Theory + computation</p></div>
  <div>
    <h2 id="codes-heading">Hyperbolic quantum error correction</h2>
    <p>Hyperbolic lattices offer a route to quantum codes with unusual geometric and information-theoretic properties. My work builds systematic methods for constructing topological CSS and Floquet codes on periodic hyperbolic lattices, identifying logical operators, and benchmarking logical performance under realistic noise.</p>
    <ul class="tag-list" aria-label="Methods used">
      <li>Stabilizer codes</li><li>Floquet codes</li><li>MWPM decoding</li><li>Monte Carlo simulation</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-feature" aria-labelledby="mbqc-heading">
  <div class="research-feature__label"><span>02</span><p>Fault tolerance</p></div>
  <div>
    <h2 id="mbqc-heading">Hyperbolic cluster states for MBQC</h2>
    <p>I study measurement-based quantum-computing architectures in which hyperbolic geometry shapes the resource state, logical encoding, and decoding problem. The goal is to identify scalable routes to fault-tolerant computation while making efficient use of physical qubits and measurements.</p>
    <p><a class="text-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27004">Read the preprint <span aria-hidden="true">→</span></a></p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-feature" aria-labelledby="simulation-heading">
  <div class="research-feature__label"><span>03</span><p>Experiment</p></div>
  <div>
    <h2 id="simulation-heading">Quantum simulation in curved geometries</h2>
    <p>In collaboration with researchers at the Institute for Quantum Computing and Julius-Maximilians-Universität Würzburg, I investigate superconducting and topoelectric circuit platforms for emulating quantum physics in hyperbolic and other non-Euclidean spaces.</p>
    <p><a class="text-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23827">Explore the circuit framework <span aria-hidden="true">→</span></a></p>
  </div>
</section>

    <h2 id="software-heading">HQECC-Threshold</h2>
